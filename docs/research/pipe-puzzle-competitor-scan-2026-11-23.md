# Pipe-rotation puzzle competitor scan

**Evidence label:** Observed listing (per [research README](README.md), item 1). Public product pages only. Not evidence of demand, keyword ranking, revenue, install counts, or market weakness.
**Checked:** 2026-11-23, via public web pages fetched without logging in or submitting forms.
**Scope:** Mobile games whose core mechanic is rotating or connecting pipe, water or flow tiles, using U.S. App Store pages where available and Google Play pages where they could be opened.
**Gap this fills:** the earlier repository note recorded that no pipe-game incumbents were catalogued.

## Method and sampling limits

- Discovery came from web search results, then each App Store page was fetched once. Fields are copied only where the page showed them. Anything not shown is `unknown`.
- Several App Store pages were fetched from non-U.S. storefront paths, which the search engine returned. These are marked **(non-US storefront)** and are not U.S. observations.
- **Google Play pages were largely truncated in the fetch tool.** Only titles and package names were readable for the two Play listings below. Play download bands, ratings and ad or purchase labels are `unknown` for all entries. A Play search page was also fetched, but its ratings came from a truncated, summarized page. They are not individually verified and are not reported here.
- Ratings counts are small or absent for most listings. Many show "not enough ratings to display an overview", so no rating is shown.
- Review themes come from one to four visible reviews per listing. They are not a representative sample and should not be used as an unmet-need finding under the README's item 4 standard.
- "Last updated" dates shown as relative phrases ("2 days ago") are recorded as shown and are not converted to calendar dates.
- No search popularity was measured. No keyword volume is implied.

## Analysis

### (a) Table stakes in this genre

- **Core loop:** tap or drag to rotate tiles on a small grid until a source connects to outlets or a drain. Virtually every listing says this.
- **Volume and structure:** hundreds of levels, or random boards, plus stages or packs. Several add daily puzzles.
- **Hints and undo-style help:** a hint that shows the solved layout appears in at least three listings. Some sell it.
- **Failure conditions:** a timer, a move or turn limit, or a spill or overflow. Relaxed or untimed modes appear in roughly a third of listings.
- **Monetization:** most free titles combine ads with an ad-removal purchase. Paid or IAP-gated hints and extra turns are common.
- **Offline play:** frequently stated.
- **Accessibility:** almost every listing says the developer has not indicated accessibility features. This is consistent across the sample.

### (b) Whether an incumbent ties a puzzle choice to the next puzzle or to a remembered village

**Not observed** in any of the 15 listings. Nothing on the pages describes a choice that changes the next puzzle, a persistent village, or a consequence that carries across levels. The closest items are:

- Light narrative framing (rescuing trapped people, watering flowers), with no described persistence.
- XP, coins and star ratings that reward performance but do not alter the world.
- Random generation (Pipes) and daily puzzles (Pipe Panic, Pipes: Connect the Flow), which are not described as responding to player choices.

These are listing-page observations only. In-game behavior was not played, so "not observed" does not establish that no game has such a layer.

### (c) Observable gaps relevant to adults 45+

These gaps come from listing text and a few reviews. They are not validated with the 45+ audience, and no age data was observed.

- **Accessibility declarations are absent** in nearly every listing. The sample cannot show whether any incumbent offers adjustable size, contrast, or pacing options.
- **Timers and turn or move limits are common.** Untimed play exists (Pipes, Water Pipe Connection Puzzle, Corner Connect, Pipe Mania Relax mode), so untimed play is a present option, not an uncovered gap. A product whose pace is adjustable and transparent would still need to be tested against these.
- **Ads and paywalled hints** appear in many free titles. Visible reviews call out ad frequency, lost wins after ads, and data collection concerns.
- **Device-specific complaints** appear for iPad and rotation in one listing.
- **Sparse reviews.** Most listings have no rating or a single rating, so there is little public evidence of what players of any age find frustrating.

### (d) Limits of this scan

- Web listings are not a physical-device storefront audit. Rankings, visibility and search results were not checked on an iPhone or in-store search.
- Two listings are from non-U.S. storefronts (Flippy Pipe, AF; Pipes: Connect the Flow, FM). Their prices and labels reflect those storefronts.
- Google Play coverage is effectively unavailable: download bands, ratings and ad labels could not be read for any game.
- Review samples are one to four visible reviews per listing. They do not establish unmet need under the README's standard.
- No search popularity was measured. No keyword volume, install count, revenue or retention is reported or implied.
- Dates shown relatively were not converted. Listing "last updated" dates are as shown at fetch time.
- Older classic titles (for example Pipe Mania by its original developer, Pipe Dream) were not located on the stores in this scan.

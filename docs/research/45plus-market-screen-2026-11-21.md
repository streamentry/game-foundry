# Adults 45+ game-market screen — 2026-11-21

## Decision state

**The audience direction is adopted; no game is selected.** The current strategy is to test familiar mobile puzzles, word games, board games, and regional games with adults 45+, beginning with U.S. adults 50–75 and Vietnam as the regional-language hypothesis. AARP provides a 50+ aggregate, not a separate 50–75 estimate, so the exact target segment size remains unknown. A 30-query comparison is in [the research queue](45plus-30-keyword-screen-2026-11-21.csv): 10 adult-oriented familiar puzzles in the U.S., 10 Vietnamese regional/traditional game queries, and 10 specialized logic puzzles in the U.S.

## What the evidence supports

### Audience reach and motivation — U.S. adults 50+

AARP's October 2026 study estimates that 60.2 million U.S. adults 50+ play video games monthly (49%). Its quantitative sample included 2,550 adults 50+, including 1,014 gamers. Among older gamers, 66% used smartphones as a play device, 79% usually played alone, 65% reported typical sessions of 15–60 minutes, 75% played to relax, and 73% played to keep their minds sharp. These are self-reported reach, device, session, and motivation data; they do not establish which mobile game keyword they search or prove a health effect. The study does not isolate ages 50–75, and fit for ages 45–49 remains unknown. [AARP study](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2026-10-22-aarp-gaming-study/)

The 2023 AARP study reported that 69% of surveyed 50+ gamers felt games were not designed with them in mind. It also identified ad nuisance, forced ad/purchase progression, notifications, and complexity as complaints. This is a strong problem signal, but it is U.S. self-report data from 2022, not evidence that today's top puzzle apps have the same gaps or that users will switch. [AARP 2023 research and methods](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/social-leisure/activities-interests/2023-gamers-50-plus/)

### Category demand and acquisition conditions

AppMagic estimates puzzle downloads rose from 7.0 billion in 2024 to 7.7 billion in 2025 (+9.9%), with puzzle IAP revenue up 7.6%. This is broad global category activity, not older-player demand or indie opportunity. The same report describes a mature match-3 segment and concentrated revenue in some subgenres. [AppMagic 2026 report](https://appmagic.rocks/files/view/upload/Reports/EN_MobileMarkeLandscape2026.pdf)

Sensor Tower estimates overall mobile-game downloads fell 12% to 24 billion in H1 2026 while IAP revenue fell 2%. This makes organic discovery harder even when a specific subgenre is growing. [Sensor Tower H1 2026 index](https://sensortower.com/blog/h1-2026-digital-gaming-market-index)

Newzoo's November 2026 localization analysis covers PC/console, 5,581 games in 38 markets and 22 languages, plus Steam language listings. It cannot validate mobile search volume or App Store competition. Use it to form a regional-language hypothesis only. [Newzoo localization study](https://new.newzoo.com/reports/game-localization-in-2026)

## Audit shortlist — hypotheses only

The first three physical storefront audits should cover one query from each research group:

1. **`large print word search` — U.S.** (1K–10K, Medium advertiser competition). The query directly matches legibility, but current listings already offer large print, adjustable text, offline play, and no-timer modes. Verify actual top-10 positions and whether recent reviews show a repeated unsolved need.
2. **`nối từ` — Vietnam.** (10K–100K, Low advertiser competition). Direct apps have varied ratings and visible word-quality complaints, but a 50K+ download Android competitor recently expanded its dictionary in response. Test residual complaint frequency and lexicon rights/quality; age-45+ fit is unknown.
3. **`shikaku puzzle` — U.S.** (1K–10K, Low advertiser competition, +900% YoY). Multiple direct Shikaku apps already exist, including Voodoo at 1.1K ratings and several newer products. Verify whether those apps occupy top organic positions or whether the search demand is not converting into downloads.

This is a shortlist for evidence collection, not three production selections. None has verified organic rank, organic installs, or 45+ cohort retention yet.

## Decision and next gate

**No prototype yet.** The evidence supports testing familiar, accessible mobile games with U.S. adults 50+, but it does not separately establish the 50–75 segment size, best game, or a low-competition keyword. A specific accessible-market promise is already offered by current listings, so differentiation must come from a measured search need and repeated competitor failure.

The required physical-storefront rank audit has not run. In this workspace, `devicectl` timed out and ADB failed to return a device list, so all competition evidence above is public catalog evidence, not organic search rank.

For the first commercial experiment, treat **1,000 non-branded organic installs in 60 days** as the proposed goal from the strategy direction, not a forecast or industry norm. Define a numeric Day-7 retention threshold before launch. Do not adopt either as a go/no-go benchmark until the research and experiment plan explain how it will be measured.

Prototype only if a shortlisted term combines measured demand, a repeated player problem, an original product wedge, commercial content rights, and a viable test cohort from the intended 50–75 segment. The next required input is a connected iPhone configured for the U.S. or Vietnam storefront; then capture ten unsponsored results and review samples for the three audit terms.

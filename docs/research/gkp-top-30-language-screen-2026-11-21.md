# Google Keyword Planner: top-30 language screen — 2026-11-21

> Historical first-pass roster. Superseded as the active discovery plan by the [45+ 30-keyword screen](45plus-market-screen-2026-11-21.md), which compares game-intent queries across three categories. This file remains an unmeasured language-market queue.

## Status

**No game is selected.** The scope is now a first-pass demand screen across the 30 largest language groups by total speakers in Ethnologue's 2026 table. This ranks people who speak a language; it does not rank game buyers, mobile users, store reach, or revenue. The queries are not age-segmented and do not establish demand among adults aged 45+.

The report uses one representative country per language. Those are comparison anchors, not totals for all speakers. Several languages need a proxy or special handling: Nigerian Pidgin has no single standard written search form; Modern Standard Arabic is mainly formal/written and Arabic searches are dialect-shaped; Punjabi and Urdu overlap; Yue and Wu are not separable by ordinary written Chinese queries. Google Planner coverage for mainland China and Iran must be confirmed before using any volume.

## Language and seed queue

The exact country, Planner language filter, and four draft seed phrases are in [the CSV queue](gkp-top-30-language-seeds-2026-11-21.csv). The seeds cover word play, logic puzzles, offline play, and daily puzzles. They are initial translations, not verified native search phrases. The queue marks this explicitly.

## Evidence collected so far

- **Measured in Google Keyword Planner:** only the earlier Vietnam screen. It showed `nối từ` at 10K–100K average monthly Google searches, `nối từ online` and `game nối từ` at 1K–10K each, and `nối chữ tiếng Việt` at 10–100. Settings were Vietnam, Vietnamese, Google Search, November 2025–October 2026. These are broad ranges, include close variants, and measure web search. They are not app-store installs. The earlier capture has no completed CSV export.
- **Not yet measured:** the other 29 language-market pairs and the four cross-language seed classes. Blank or missing measures are **unknown**, not zero.
- **Age fit:** none of these broad search measures identifies the searcher's age. Demand from adults 45+ remains **unknown** until supported by direct audience evidence or age-segmented product data.
- **Google account access:** the current Keyword Planner research handoff is waiting for Google sign-in to be saved in the secure browser card. No campaign was created and no spend occurred.
- **Competition:** Google Ads competition is advertiser competition. It cannot establish low App Store competition. The store's top organic results and current mixed-sentiment reviews still need a physical storefront audit.

Google describes average monthly searches as dependent on the selected time, location and network, and says close variants are included. Its competition field describes advertisers ([historical metrics](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3022575?hl=en), [Keyword Planner guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243?hl=en)).

## Research design

1. Query the four language-specific seeds in each representative country, with a common network and date range. Save the observed ranges, Google Ads competition, and trend columns, including zero-volume rows where Planner actually returns them.
2. Keep the original query and every Planner suggestion together. Add `app`, `download`, `offline`, and mechanic-specific terms only after the first result set; do not compare a broad category word to an app-intent phrase as if they were equivalent.
3. Native-check the best suggestions before shortlisting. The initial translations are a discovery aid, not a localization deliverable.
4. Shortlist a small number of language-market pairs using search intent and trend, then audit their actual iOS/Android storefront results and recent reviews. Only that step can support a competition or unmet-need claim.
5. Keep country-level estimates separate. Do not add totals across markets if Google close variants, language overlap, diaspora, or bilingual queries may duplicate intent.

## Current lead and counterargument

Vietnamese Nối Từ remains the only specific lead with a Google search range and visible store-review complaints. The strongest objection remains that its search volume may represent rules or web play, while existing apps already cover the core loop and the lexicon rights/quality problem is unresolved. It is a Vietnam audit lead, not the global first-game decision.

## Next action and stop rule

After Google sign-in is saved, finish the common 30-language seed screen. Then choose at most three market-keyword pairs for physical store audits. Stop or replace a candidate if Planner intent is mostly unrelated to mobile play, the storefront is already served by strong direct games, or a real differentiator cannot be verified. Do not build from speaker counts or low advertiser competition alone.

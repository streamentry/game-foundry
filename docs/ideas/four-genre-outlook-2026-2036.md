# Four genre bets for 2026–2036: Simulation, RPG, Strategy, Puzzle

> Added 2026-11-22 from founder-supplied perspective. This is a **speculative genre research backlog**, not a verified market forecast or production approval. No evidence is presented that exactly these four genres will dominate, that all scripted RPGs will disappear, or that puzzle games lead daily active users. Verify before using as investment assumptions.

## Core hypothesis: two ends of the opportunity spectrum

1. **Hyper-accessible evergreen loops**: offline-friendly, understandable in seconds, 2–5 minute play; competing for attention with short-form media.
2. **Persistent AI-enabled social worlds**: co-created places, narratives and economies where play and social interaction blend. Substantially higher live-ops, moderation, server and trust costs.

The two extremes require different business models and organizational capabilities. Do **not** assume they belong in one game.

## Four genre theses

| Genre | Proposed direction | Relevant reference examples (not evidence of future success) | Main cost/risk | Indie-friendly wedge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Simulation** | Persistent co-created worlds, creator ecosystems, NPCs with bounded memory, optional economies | Roblox, Fortnite UEFN, inZOI | Hosting, safety, NPC reliability, content moderation, network effects | Small offline micro-ecosystem or one memorable NPC before any multiuser world |
| **Role-playing** | AI-assisted emergent conversations and consequences; optional cross-platform continuity | Genshin Impact, Honkai, Wuthering Waves; tabletop RPGs | Hallucinations, narrative state drift, model costs, IP and trust, AAA production expectations | Small detective or social-deduction story with auditable state machine |
| **Strategy** | Hybrid-casual, one-touch tactical loop with deeper upgrades, deckbuilding or async competition | Last War: Survival, Whiteout Survival, Balatro, Slay the Spire | Pay-to-win incentives, systemic balance, progression bloat, paid acquisition | Small async tactics or deckbuilding roguelite with offline core |
| **Puzzle** | Immediate tactile spatial/merge play, shared daily challenges, optional adaptive practice | Block Blast!, Royal Match, NYT Wordle / Connections | Saturation, low differentiated search demand, cloning, ad dependence | Wordless daily mechanic with a crisp reason to switch |

**Screen selection mapping:** Simulation, Role-Playing, Strategy, Puzzle. This is an exploration shortlist, **not a recommendation to invest equally in all four**.

## Decision framework

- **Distribution:** Puzzle has plausible ASO/organic wedge; simulation and RPG depend on content, community or creator distribution; social worlds have severe cold starts. Test actual channel, not just genre popularity.
- **Audience:** Existing studio default is adults 45+. Do not assume Gen Z/Alpha simulation behavior generalizes to that segment. Split audiences and apply explicit recruitment.
- **AI:** Test with rules and state machines first. Only add a model when it produces measured enjoyment/retention gains that exceed inference, latency, safety and QA costs. NPC persistent memory requires user consent, deletion and bounded personal data.
- **Retention:** Daily puzzles can benefit from a shared fixed puzzle; personalized practice should remain optional to protect fairness and common experience.
- **Economics:** Before live services, estimate infrastructure, moderation, abuse handling, support and live-ops. Avoid speculative token economies as a default.
- **Market claims to verify:** Genre DAU rankings, youth social-world adoption, franchise revenue durability, hybrid-casual economics, model-driven retention, and cross-platform demand. None are established merely by the examples above.

## Cheapest staged test

1. **Puzzle baseline**: compare Unfold / Still / existing Kakuro Compass against incumbents with real target players.
2. **Strategy probe**: prototype Three Cards or One Turn Kingdom only after finding a concrete player/distribution hypothesis.
3. **Narrative probe**: test a single authored detective case, then A/B constrained AI dialogue if the authored game works.
4. **Simulation probe**: test one offline micro-ecosystem or NPC; **do not** build a social platform for the first experiment.

**Stop:** no repeated voluntary play, no observable competitor preference, no credible distribution channel, or safety/operating costs inconsistent with indie economics. **Continue:** clearer comprehension, measurable return, explicit switching motivation, and plausible acquisition and maintenance.

**Strongest contrary view:** The cited franchises win through distribution, capital, creative ecosystems and network effects—properties a small studio cannot copy simply by reproducing a genre. For this repository, genre labels are discovery filters; player behavior and viable acquisition decide what ships.

# Ten-year global gaming thesis and seven product briefs (2027–2036)

> Added 2026-11-22 from founder-supplied strategic notes. **Hypotheses, not validated forecasts or build approvals.** This complements the [50 global concepts](global-concepts-2027-2036.md) and the existing [100 search hypotheses](candidates.csv); it does not create duplicate game IDs or override the existing roadmap.

## North star

**Make people feel smarter, calmer, or more connected.** Combine timeless play with simple, accessible interaction. Hypothesis: daily puzzle + casual play + optional, *invisible* personalization can produce enduring games across cultures and generations. Examples motivating the thesis: Tetris (spatial mastery), Minecraft (creation and ownership), Wordle (shared daily ritual). These are analogies, not predictions of comparable outcomes.

**Two incompatible starting strategies, both worth testing independently:**
- **Search-led indie wedge:** improve a known niche with proven demand and observable competitor shortcomings (candidate: Kakuro).
- **Category creation:** invent a compelling mechanic and distribute via sharing, repeat play, and brand (candidates: Unfold / Still).

A game can be compelling but undiscoverable. Product, distribution, monetization, and player retention must be validated together.

## Three strategic bets and their different tests

| Goal | Candidate | Cheapest credible first test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search-led organic discovery | Kakuro Zen / existing Kakuro Compass | Official locale-specific search popularity + top-10 listing/review audit; comparative usability |
| New globally understandable mechanic | Unfold | 5–10 deterministic levels, first 30-second comprehension and incumbent preference |
| Durable calm consumer brand | Still | Tactile physics vertical slice, observed repeat play and fair-failure interviews |

**Do not build a generalized AI platform first.** Start without backend, account or generative service. Adaptive practice is a separate hypothesis: fixed global daily puzzle for shared conversation, personalized *optional practice* for difficulty fit. Test retention lift against nonadaptive control; record privacy and fairness tradeoffs.

## Seven-day falsification sprint (proposed; not executed)

1. **Demand screen:** Review 30–50 nonbranded App Store queries across US, Japan, Germany and Brazil, segmented by locale; collect official popularity where accessible, top results, negative-review patterns and actual market evidence. Do not substitute downloads for keyword searches.
2. **Mechanics:** Build/playtest tiny isolated vertical slices of **Unfold**, **Still** and the already-existing **Kakuro Compass** (evaluate if a Zen redesign has incremental value). Reuse existing prototypes rather than cloning them.
3. **Behavior:** Recruit 30–50 relevant participants *per prototype* if feasible, including the target older-adult segment where claimed. Observe day-0 comprehension, voluntary replay, D1 return, D7 return, and head-to-head preference versus a polished incumbent. Small samples provide directional evidence only, not robust estimates.
4. **Predeclared screening heuristics:** D1 >= 30% and D7 >= 15% are **founder-proposed exploratory thresholds, not industry baselines**. Record actual denominator, uncertainty, channel and player mix; avoid declaring success from tiny samples.
5. **Stop / continue:** Park if comprehension is poor or players cannot articulate a reason to switch; stop production expansion when acquisition is unproven or players prefer incumbents. Continue only after observing repeat play **and** a viable distribution route.

## Central counterargument

AI makes game development cheaper for *everyone*. A novel mechanic without defensible distribution is not a moat. Potential durable advantages: memorable brand identity, recurring rituals, independently verified level quality, organic sharing, accessibility, coherent premium experience, and player trust. AI personalization is an **unproven mechanism**, not the value proposition.

**Prediction confidence:** relatively high that simple puzzles remain relevant; low that adaptive play becomes its own winning category; extremely low in any named title becoming a top global game in 2036.

**Decision checkpoint:** No production greenlight merely because concepts were documented. Seek external player behavior before more content, features or backlog proliferation.

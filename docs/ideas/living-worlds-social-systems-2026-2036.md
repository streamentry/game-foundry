# Living worlds, social systems and personalized games: 2026–2036 thesis

> Captured from founder-supplied strategic prediction on 2026-11-22. **This is a prospective investment/design thesis, not independently verified market evidence.** The quoted 4–10× AI productivity estimate attributed to Tim Sweeney and broader forecasts remain unverified here. No production approval or new market-sized claims.

## Main proposition

Future cultural trendsetters may be **reactive, persistent, player-shaped worlds**, not just more authored content. The strongest territory is the overlap of **simulation, role-playing, asynchronous/cooperative systems and creation tools**. Mobile can still dominate player volume without necessarily generating every cultural/design first.

The critical distinction: an AI system that produces more content is not necessarily a world players feel is alive. Persistent **causality, memory, agency and social consequences** matter more than sheer text quantity.

## Five structural trends — hypotheses to test

| Trend | Founder-supplied hypothesis | What could be true instead | Research priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Generative and runtime AI | AI accelerates production and powers NPC memory, emergent narrative, economies and evolving worlds | Development bottlenecks shift to design, QA, safety, discovery and consistency; authored content retains premium value | Compare deterministic/scripted world with AI-assisted equivalent, including cost |
| Social co-created spaces | Shared persistence and belonging outperform grind-based live service | Single-player mastery and curated narrative still dominate many motivations; multiplayer suffers cold start | Observe voluntary group creation and referrals |
| Cloud/mobile/cross-play | Cloud lowers hardware barriers and makes high-end worlds widely accessible | Latency, data cost, battery and hardware access limit adoption; mobile native remains preferable | Test player willingness to stream/switch devices and abandonment |
| Systems simulation | AI makes economies, societies and ecosystems more dynamic | AI agents create instability, opaque outcomes and runaway hosting costs | Test causal readability and meaningful player interventions |
| UGC + creators | AI lowers creation friction and creates self-sustaining creator economies | Content flooding increases discovery friction, moderation and reward concentration | Measure publish → recipient play → remix, not prompt generations |

**Shorter-term watchlist:** extraction shooters, roguelites, blockbuster open-world action, polished narrative RPGs. **Longer-term hypothesis:** hybridized genres become more common; genre purity does not necessarily disappear.

## Four proposed global trendsetter archetypes

| Archetype | Intended player outcome | Core game loop | Feasible indie-sized experiment | Existing backlog overlap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **AI-native living world** | The world appears to remember and respond | Player action → NPC goals/memory → world consequences → meaningful next choice | 3-NPC village with visible motives and deterministic event log; optional LLM dialogue | GX-002 Neighbor Memory, GX-005 Traveling Tavern, GX-014 Companion, GC-021 Little Worlds |
| **Persistent social systems sandbox** | Belonging and co-authored community stories | Build or gather → negotiate/coordinate → shared environment evolves → return together | Two-player asynchronous town ecology with a shared event history, no economy or public server | GX-003 Tiny Market, GX-013 Infinite Worlds, GC-036 The Village |
| **Deep hybrid-casual mobile** | A 2-minute entry with strategic depth over weeks | Instantly readable action → consequence → progression / tactical choice → later return | Single-screen spatial loop that gradually earns 3 economic decisions; compare without meta layer | GX-007 One Turn Kingdom, GX-009 Three Cards, GC-003 One Move |
| **AI-assisted creator platform** | Turn ideas into playable, shared experiences without learning a complex tool | Describe/assemble → edit → publish → friends play/remix → iterate | Template-based room builder with editable output and share link, *no* generative 3D backend | GX-013 Infinite Worlds, GC-021 Little Worlds |

These are **four research territories, not four newly approved production titles**.

## Proposed mechanic probes (extensions, not duplicated candidates)

**LW-01 — Causal memory village**: Three deterministic NPCs each have visible goals and relationship state. Player changes a shared resource; downstream NPC choices should be explainable. Experiment: can players predict and care about consequences? *Failure:* behavior feels random or unfair. Extend GX-002, do not create a competing NPC codebase.

**LW-02 — Shared settlement story**: Two players contribute actions asynchronously, triggering persistent consequences on one small map. Experiment: compare cooperative return against the same game played solo. *Failure:* invite and coordination friction exceed social benefit. Extend GC-036/GX-003.

**LW-03 — Hybrid micro-loop**: A 60-second tactile puzzle feeds one deliberate settlement/strategy choice; the meta-game must change how people approach the next round. Experiment: randomized comparison against puzzle alone. *Failure:* no replay uplift or meta layer obscures puzzle joy. Extend GC-003/GX-007.

**LW-04 — Human-guided world creator**: A constrained prompt generates a playable editable room. Humans can inspect, correct and share every output. Experiment: percentage of recipients who actually play and remix versus manual template authoring. *Failure:* output unreliable, share recipients ignore it, or moderation cost dominates. Extend GX-013.

Use the `LW-*` prefixes as **tests within existing initiatives**, not new production game IDs.

## Design principles for systems that feel alive

1. **State persists and is legible:** goals, memories and consequences should remain coherent, with player-accessible explanation and recovery.
2. **Players have agency:** their actions alter observable future possibilities, rather than receiving decorative AI dialogue.
3. **Controlled emergence:** deterministic core rules govern resources and progression; models may suggest dialogue or events within validated boundaries.
4. **Craft before infinity:** a few genuinely interesting interactions are better than endless low-quality generated text.
5. **Social trust:** consent, moderation, abuse prevention and anti-harassment features precede open UGC or shared economies.
6. **Human-readable economics:** never rely on financialized tokens, manipulative monetization, or artificial social obligation for retention.
7. **Privacy:** memory is opt-in, scoped, inspectable and deletable; avoid profiling by default.
8. **Cross-platform only where useful:** test phone-native interaction and actual switching behavior before adding cloud/VR.

## Opposition and assumptions

**Strongest opposing case:** Spectacular AI systems may be *less* commercially valuable than polished, fixed-rule games because predictability, competitive fairness, curation and low operating cost are core sources of enjoyment. Social platforms also concentrate around entrenched network effects.

**Load-bearing assumptions:**
- Players repeatedly prefer *systemic reactivity* over carefully authored reactivity, when compared blind.
- Inference/hosting/moderation costs are manageable at observed revenue and usage.
- Discovery and network effects can start from a small, single-player-or-two-player wedge.

**Non-consensus caution:** The assertion that puzzle, board, card and word games become mere side features is too confident. They may remain stand-alone commercial winners even if living worlds become the prestige frontier. The observation that some players dislike battle-pass grind does not establish a global preference shift.

## Seven-day feasibility and evidence test (not executed)

1. Select one archetype and one measurable player need, with a named target audience. Default Game Foundry emphasis remains adults 45+, and it is unknown whether that cohort prefers these systems.
2. Build/extend **one** tiny local-first prototype (ideally LW-01: 3 NPCs, finite state and 10 meaningful choices) using deterministic rules first.
3. Record concrete state transitions and failures, then optionally replace only dialogue surface with bounded AI.
4. Give 10–20 target users the prototype and a crafted/scripted control; observe comprehension, voluntary repeat, remembered consequences, and preference. This is exploratory, not statistical validation.
5. Estimate per-active-user generation, storage, moderation and live-ops costs. Stop if the system is not substantially more enjoyable, reliably causal or affordable; continue to larger playtest only if return and preference signals emerge.

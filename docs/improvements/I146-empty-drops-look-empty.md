# I146 — Empty water drops look empty

Date: 2026-11-25. Branch: feat/mosselora-i127-plus.

## Player (two Haiku walk-throughs, b136 and b152)

- b136: "Nothing says what [the drops] count: moves left, water for the garden, or lives."
- b152, after I130's flights: "the water-drop bar shows two rows of drops with no label. **I can't tell how many I have left** or what they're for."

Both read the meter as a stock that runs down. Looking at it cold, I see why: since I105 the dry drops were **solid dark slate**, the heaviest, "fullest" shapes on the screen, while the wet drops were pale aqua. A full row of dark drops looks like a full tank of lives. The flight (I130) explains it in motion, but most of the time the meter is still.

## PM decision

**Empty drops look empty.** A dry slot becomes the droplet's **outline with a faint fill** (slate, ≥3:1 against paper, as I105 required), and a wet slot is the full painted aqua droplet. The row now reads as "slots still to fill" and can only grow. Still no caption.

## Dev

- `tools/evergrove_art_pipeline.py`: a new `hollow()` derives `glyph-droplet-empty` from the painted droplet (alpha minus a 15px erosion for a ring, plus a 14% inner fill, in white for tinting). Every other sprite regenerated byte-identical.
- `MosseloraArt.DropletEmpty`. `EvergroveApp.SetDrop(image, wet)` swaps sprite and tint: wet is the painted drop in white tint; dry is the hollow sprite in slate `#4D5954`, falling back to the old solid slate if the sprite is missing. `PaintDrops`, the solve fill and the flight arrival (I130) all go through it.

## Result (b154, iPhone 17 simulator, scripted tour)

`I146-empty-drops.jpg` (gitignored), bottom row, `16-winter-village` b153 compared with b154: two rows of dark solid drops become two filled aqua drops followed by fourteen hollow outlines. The 3×3 meters change the same way. The scripted tour captures all 20 screens. Verify passes.

**Not verified:** the outline's contrast measured on a device screen (the slate is darker than I105's solid fill to keep a thin line ≥3:1), and whether players now read it as progress. Re-ask the same cold-read question.

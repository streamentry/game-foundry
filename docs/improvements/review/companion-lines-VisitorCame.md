# Companion lines: VisitorCame

**Status: approved by the founder on 2026-11-27 (every line), and moved into `games/evergrove/Assets/Resources/Evergrove/Lines/en.json`.** Written by hand (no script) for smart companion SC04. This file stays as the review record; `en.json` is what ships.

**The fact:** the visitor a keeper's good neighbours bring has found your garden: Mira's owl (a grove), Orin's heron (something built by the water), Fen's hedgehog (a flower sheltered by a tree).

**Slots:** none. Each voice speaks only of its own visitor.

**Writing notes:** Lines follow the real rules (`Visitors.Why`). The owl comes at night. The heron fishes by day, but not when the river is frozen. The hedgehog comes at dusk and sleeps the winter through. Lines speak of the visitor's habit ("comes at dusk"), not a sighting now, because the remark may fall in winter. Animal facts are true ones.

## Mira (voice 0)

1. An owl has found your grove. It comes at night, when the garden's quiet.
2. There's an owl that visits your grove after dark. Two trees side by side were all it needed.
3. Your grove has an owl now. My grandmother said an owl in the trees means the trees are content.
4. An owl's taken to your grove. You might hear it calling, after dark.
5. Owls like a grove to perch in, and yours suits one.
6. Your grove has a night visitor: an owl. It keeps the mice honest.
7. An owl comes to your trees at night. Owls are very good at being quiet; you have to listen.
8. I'm glad the owl found your grove. Owls choose carefully.
9. There's an owl in your grove some nights. Its feathers are so soft it flies without a sound.
10. Your grove brought an owl. Two trees together, and the night creatures notice.
11. The owl in your grove can turn its head almost all the way round. I tried once. I couldn't.
12. An owl visits your grove. My mother would leave the window open to hear one.
13. Your trees have an owl now, after dark. It's a good sign for a garden.
14. If you're ever here at night, listen for the owl in your grove.
15. An owl calls from your grove at night. Low and soft, like someone asking a question.
16. The owl in your grove sleeps through the day, tucked against a trunk.
17. Your grove has become an owl's place. That takes trees that stand together.
18. There's an owl that thinks your grove is the best spot in the valley. I agree with it.
19. An owl visiting your grove: my grandmother would have called that luck.
20. Owls hunt by listening. Your grove must be a good place to listen from.

## Orin (voice 1)

1. A heron fishes by what you built at the water. Patient bird.
2. There's a heron that stands by your build at the water. Still as a post, for ages.
3. A heron's found your work by the water. It knows a good spot when it sees one.
4. The heron by your build at the water stands on one leg. I've tried. Not for long.
5. Herons like something built beside the water to stand near. Yours suits one.
6. A heron comes by day to fish beside what you built. Better company than most.
7. Your build by the water has a regular: a grey heron, very serious.
8. That heron by your waterside work is the most patient creature in the valley.
9. A heron fishes near your build at the water. It doesn't hurry, and it rarely misses.
10. Your work by the water has a heron for a neighbour. Good neighbours, herons. Quiet.
11. The heron that fishes by your build flies with its neck folded back, like a coat on a hook.
12. A heron stands guard by what you built at the water. I'd hire it.
13. There's a heron by your build at the water. When the river freezes it goes elsewhere. Sensible bird.
14. Herons need still water and a quiet place to stand. Your build by the river gives them both.
15. A heron's made your waterside build part of its round. That's an honour, in heron terms.
16. The heron by your build is up and fishing early. Earlier than me.
17. A heron fishes beside your work at the water. It treats the place with respect.
18. You've a heron by your waterside build. My father used to call a heron the river's foreman.
19. That heron by your build at the water: grey, tall, and never in a rush.
20. A heron comes to your build by the water. Good work makes a good perch.

## Fen (voice 2)

1. A hedgehog comes to your sheltered flower at dusk. They know a safe spot.
2. There's a hedgehog that visits the flower you put beside a tree. At dusk, mostly.
3. A hedgehog's found your sheltered flower. They snuffle about like little grumbling bears.
4. Your flower beside the tree has a visitor at dusk: a hedgehog.
5. Hedgehogs like a flower with a tree beside it. Yours suits one perfectly.
6. A hedgehog comes snuffling round your sheltered flower in the evenings.
7. There's a hedgehog that thinks your sheltered flower is the best spot in the garden.
8. A hedgehog visits your flower by the tree. In winter it sleeps the cold away, somewhere snug.
9. Your sheltered flower brought a hedgehog. They eat the slugs, so the flowers are grateful.
10. A hedgehog comes at dusk to your flower beside the tree. I leave a shallow dish of water out for it.
11. Hedgehogs have thousands of spines. The one by your sheltered flower wears every one of them.
12. A hedgehog has made your sheltered flower part of its evening walk.
13. There's a hedgehog by your flower near the tree. It rolls into a ball if you get too close.
14. Your flower beside the tree has a prickly friend. A hedgehog, at dusk.
15. A hedgehog by your sheltered flower. They walk a long way of an evening, and yours is on the route.
16. I watched the hedgehog by your sheltered flower for ages. So busy, for something so small.
17. Your sheltered flower is a hedgehog's stopping place. They like a bit of cover overhead.
18. A hedgehog visits at dusk, right by your flower and its tree. Quiet as you like.
19. There's a hedgehog in your garden. It comes to the flower you sheltered with a tree.
20. Hedgehogs snuffle and snort as they go. You can hear the one by your sheltered flower before you see it.

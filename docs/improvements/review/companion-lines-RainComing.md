**The fact:** rain is falling upstream today and reaches the garden tomorrow (the garden's own weather tomorrow is rainy). Orin, who minds the bridge and gutters, says it.

**Slots:** none.

**Writing notes:** every line says both halves truly: rain upstream now, here tomorrow. Practical, Orin's way.

## Orin (voice 1)

1. Rain on the hills upstream. It'll be with us by tomorrow.
2. The river's carrying news: rain upstream today, here tomorrow.
3. I can see rain on the far hills. It'll reach the garden tomorrow; I've checked the gutters.
4. Rain upstream today. Tomorrow it's our turn. I'll get the tools under cover.
5. There's weather up the valley. Rain, coming our way by tomorrow.
6. Rain's falling upstream. The river will be up a little tomorrow, and so will the puddles.
7. Tomorrow's rain is already on the hills. Good warning, a river.
8. The hills upstream are grey with rain. We'll have it here tomorrow.
9. Rain upstream. I'll clear the bridge drains today, so tomorrow's rain has somewhere to go.
10. Rain on the high ground today, rain in the garden tomorrow. The river tells you first.
11. My father could smell rain a day off. I just look upstream. It's coming, tomorrow.
12. Rain upstream today. The garden will have a drink tomorrow.
13. There's rain over the hills where the river starts. It'll find us by tomorrow.
14. Upstream's getting wet today. We'll get our share tomorrow.
15. The river's a day ahead of the weather. Rain upstream now, here tomorrow.
16. Rain on the hills. I've stacked the timber under the eaves, ready for tomorrow.
17. Rain's on its way down the valley. Tomorrow, I'd say.
18. I see rain upstream. Tomorrow will be a good day for indoor jobs.
19. Rain upstream today. If you've anything to dry outside, today's the day.
20. Rain upstream, here by tomorrow. The water butts will be glad.

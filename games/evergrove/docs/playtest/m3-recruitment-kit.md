## 0. Wording rules for every message

- Adult, plain, respectful tone. Say "adults aged 45 and over" only where the age criterion is needed.
- Do not use "senior", "elderly", "older players", "golden years" or any age stereotype. Do not describe the audience by its needs (eyesight, dexterity, memory).
- Do not claim or hint that the game improves memory, cognition, focus or health, and do not call it brain training.
- Call it an **early, unfinished prototype**. Never call it a launch, a finished game or a product people should buy.
- Do not describe the hypothesis (the village, the creek, consequences of choices). The screener must not reveal it ([session kit §1](m3-session-kit.md)).
- Do not promise a reward for liking the game, finishing it or saying anything in particular.

## 1. Invitation text

### 1a. Email or direct message (120 words or fewer)

Send one message per person, not a group list. Use BCC or a mail-merge tool so recipients cannot see each other.

> **Subject:** Testing an early puzzle game prototype (about 20 minutes)
>
> Hello [first name],
>
> I'm [your name], [studio name]. We are testing an early, unfinished puzzle game prototype called Mosselora. We are testing the game, not you. There is no wrong way to play.
>
> The session takes about 20 minutes: [date, time, place or video call]. [We provide the phone, or you may use your own if agreed.] Afterwards we ask a few questions. Notes use a code, not your name. Recording is optional and asked separately.
>
> You can stop at any time, skip anything, or leave without giving a reason. [GIFT LINE: TO DECIDE, see 1c]
>
> If you are interested, reply to [contact] by [deadline]. Tell us if you need larger text or breaks.
>
> Thank you,
> [your name]

*Word count of the body above is checked at 120 or fewer before use. Re-count after any edit.*

### 1b. Community post (60 words or fewer)

Use only where the group's rules allow it. Post with the group organiser's agreement, not as a cold broadcast.

> Looking for adults aged 45 and over to try an early puzzle game prototype for about 20 minutes. We are testing the game, not you. Notes are taken under a code. Recording is optional and asked separately. You can stop at any time. [Location or video] on [date]. Reply to [contact] by [deadline]. [Gift line, only if decided.]

### 1c. Thank-you gift: TO DECIDE

- **Amount and form: TO DECIDE.** The founder chooses it and buys it. This kit does not commit any spending.
- Keep it modest. A larger gift can pull people toward saying positive things about the game.
- Give the same gift to everyone who attends, whatever they think of the game and whether they finish. Do not make it conditional on completing the session, answering in a particular way, or a follow-up.
- Do not mention the gift in the invitation unless it is decided. Do not offer a higher gift to hard-to-recruit bands.

## 2. Where to recruit without bias

The aim is a mix of people who are not already comfortable with apps, puzzles or the studio. Record the channel for every person (see section 5), so the channel can be checked later.

| Channel category | Examples | Why it helps | Bias to watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Community centres | Lunch clubs, activity groups, reception notice boards | Broad mix of people, including people who rarely use apps | Volunteers may be the most sociable members. Ask the staff to post the notice, not only to pass on names. |
| Hobby groups | Walking, gardening, choir, photography, card or board game clubs | Mixed experience with phones and games | Groups skew friendly and social. Spread seats across at least two groups. |
| Puzzle or crossword clubs | Local crossword or quiz groups | Useful for the "weekly" puzzle-player seats | Strong puzzle experience. **Cap at 3 seats** so experienced puzzlers do not dominate. |
| Library notice boards and library groups | Printed flyers, library-run sessions | Free, local, reaches people who are not online | Printed flyers with a phone number and an in-person sign-up, not only a web form. |
| Neighbourhood groups | Local noticeboards, residents' associations, parish newsletters | Local and varied | Mostly reached online. Post offline as well. |
| Workplace networks outside the studio | Former colleagues of non-studio contacts, other employers' staff groups | Mixed ages and roles | Acquaintances can be encouraging. Do not recruit anyone who reports to the founder. Ask recipients not to forward the message. |
| Gaming communities and tech forums | Mobile game forums, developer groups | Easy to reach | **Not used** for M3. They are the most tech-comfortable and most enthusiastic group, which is the opposite of the target. |
| Paid advertising | Social ads with age or interest targeting | Scalable | **Not in this kit.** Platform targeting favours frequent phone users. Needs a separate founder approval and spend limit. |
| Health, care or clinic services | Clinics, care providers | Reaches people with age-related needs | **Not recommended.** It implies a health context and needs a separate ethics review. |

**General rules to reduce bias**

- Use at least **three** different channel categories.
- Cap the founder's own network at **2 seats of 12** (**TO DECIDE**). Do not use only friends or staff, as [VALIDATION Day 3](../VALIDATION.md) requires.
- Offer at least two ways to reply: a phone number, a paper form at the venue, or email.
- Do not screen out anyone for being slow with a phone, asking for larger text, or being unsure about technology. Those are the people the study needs.
- Log every reply, including people who are screened out or decline. Their rows feed the denominator in [results-template.md §2](results-template.md).

## 4. Scheduling and logistics checklist

### 4a. Gates before any session

- [ ] **M2 device build is installed and smoke-tested.** One candidate build from one source SHA, on one compact Android phone and one iPhone, per [12 §4](../specs/12-M1-M2-EXECUTION-CHECKLIST.md). M2 cannot be inferred from an M1 export or from a .NET test. [09](../specs/09-EXECUTION-CONTROL.md) records M1 and M2 as not independently verified. **No session is booked until this is done.**
- [ ] Build Git SHA and artifact hash recorded ([results-template.md §1](results-template.md)).
- [ ] Predeclared items settled and recorded in [decision-log.md](../../../../docs/research/decision-log.md): target N, quota cut-offs, professional-developer rule, hint policy, facilitator stop rule, threshold set (section 5 of the session kit), consent retention schedule, private storage location.
- [ ] Participant codes assigned from the tracking table (section 6), not from names.

### 4b. Device

- [ ] **Default: a provided phone of each class** (compact and larger), set up identically each time. Record model, OS version and screen class. **TO DECIDE**: whether to use provided phones only. Recommended for M3, since device class must stay consistent.
- [ ] **Participant's own phone: only if the founder approves** a distribution route for the research build. Check that installing it needs no account data from the participant and that it is research-only. Own phones add device variation and should be recorded as "other / unknown" or by class.
- [ ] Reset to a clean save before each session. Record the reset. Do not reuse a save from another participant.
- [ ] Charge the phone. Set brightness to a comfortable default. Record the in-game text-size setting (100, 125 or 150%) at the start. Do not change it for the participant unless they ask. Confirm that the setting works on the build being used.

### 4c. Room and comfort

- [ ] Quiet room, one facilitator, at most one note-taker. No observers who can speak.
- [ ] Seat with back support and a table at a comfortable height. Good light, no glare on the screen.
- [ ] A phone stand is available if the participant wants to hold it or rest it.
- [ ] Large-print copy of the consent sheet, and a large-print card with the facilitator's exact opening line ([session kit §3](m3-session-kit.md)).
- [ ] Reading glasses are allowed. Ask the participant to bring them if they use them.
- [ ] Water and a break are offered. A break does not end the session unless the participant chooses.

### 4d. Access needs (asked in advance)

- [ ] Ask one general question when booking: "Is there anything that would make it easier for you to take part?" Offer examples: larger text, reading glasses, hearing (speak louder, or written instructions), a seat with arms, breaks, a different time of day.
- [ ] Record the category only, for example "larger text". Do not record medical details ([session kit §1](m3-session-kit.md)).

### 4e. Timing and consent

- [ ] Book 30-minute slots: about 5 minutes for the consent script, about 20 minutes for play and questions, about 5 minutes for the exit questions.
- [ ] Read the consent script aloud ([session kit §2](m3-session-kit.md)). Record notes consent and recording consent as **two separate answers**. Recording defaults to "no".
- [ ] Record the build SHA, the chapter and the device on the observation sheet **before** the session starts.
- [ ] Use only the scripted facilitator lines ([session kit §3](m3-session-kit.md)). Any other words are an intervention and must be recorded.

### 4f. Reminders and after the session

- [ ] One reminder, 1 to 2 days before: time, place or video link, what to bring (reading glasses if used), how to cancel or reschedule. Do not ask anyone to practise or prepare.
- [ ] Write the observation sheet the same day. Record words exactly. Do not add interpretation.
- [ ] Update the tracking table (section 6).
- [ ] Give the thank-you gift as decided in 1c.
- [ ] Record any technical failure with the log. Follow the predeclared rule. Do not discard a session silently.

## 5. Privacy

### 5a. What is kept, and where

| Data | Purpose | Where it lives | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name and contact details | Scheduling only | Private store, outside the repository | Minimum needed. Keep separate from the code key. |
| Code-to-person key (for example P-01 = a name) | Links the code to the private record | Separate private file, with separate access | Never in the tracking table or any repository file. |
| Consent record: notes yes or no, recording yes or no, time, code | Shows consent was given | Private store, with the signed paper form | Record notes and recording separately. |
| Age band (not birth year) | Quotas and reporting by band | Tracking table (pseudonymous) | No birth year, date of birth or address. |
| Prior puzzle play, mobile-game play, device class, screen class, language, channel code | Quotas and denominators | Tracking table (pseudonymous) | Channel is recorded as a code. |
| Access need (category only) | Making the session workable | Private store | No medical details. |
| Observation sheet: counts, failure modes, verbatim quotes | M3 evidence | Private store | Quotes can identify people. Redact before any sharing. |
| Recordings (screen or voice) | Optional evidence | Private store only, and only with separate consent | Default: none. Never in the repository. |

### 5b. What must never go in the public repository

- Names, contact details, signed consent forms, recordings, screenshots of participants, raw quotes that identify anyone, the code-to-person key, or the follow-up list.
- Includes commits, branch names, pull request text, issue comments, build logs and CI artefacts. Check each before committing.
- Only anonymised counts with denominators, or a redacted summary, may be published ([results-template.md](results-template.md) reporting rules).

### 5c. Handling

- Send invitations one by one, or by mail merge, so recipients cannot see each other.
- Do not paste participant names, contact details or identifying quotes into any tool or service that the founder has not approved for this data.
- Use a private folder with access limited to the founder and any named researcher.

### 5d. Retention (proposal; **TO PREDECLARE**)

- Contact details: delete within [30] days after the last session, or on withdrawal if sooner. **TO DECIDE.**
- Consent records: keep for [TO DECIDE], so consent can be shown later.
- Observation sheets and the code-to-person key: keep privately until results are published. After publication, keep only the redacted version. Period: **TO DECIDE**.
- Recordings: none by default. If any exist, delete them after coding, or on withdrawal, whichever is sooner. **TO DECIDE.**
- In published tables, suppress or merge any cell that could identify a person (for example, a band with only one person).

### 5e. Withdrawal

- Any participant can withdraw at any time, before, during or after the session, by replying to [contact]. No reason is needed.
- On withdrawal, delete the private record, contact details and linked notes or recording, following [session kit §2](m3-session-kit.md). Mark the code "withdrawn (consent)" in the tracking table, with no other identifying detail.
- Tell participants in the consent conversation that counts already published in aggregate cannot be separated out.
- A next-day or later follow-up needs its own separate consent. Do not treat a session consent as covering it.
- The in-app `LocalPlaytest` report stores no identity, and no tracking SDK or analytics is added for this study ([10](../specs/10-EXPERIMENT-PROTOCOLS.md), [README](README.md)).

## Ideas and outcome

| # | Idea | Area | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Root stones: a solver gate for boards whose shape changes | Puzzle depth | **Shipped as a hidden solver gate**, not wired into play |
| 2 | The protected pipe names its cause | Truthful memory | **Shipped** |
| 3 | Facing the table (board turns 180° for the person opposite) | Family, one device | **Dropped** |
| 4 | The bloom follows the water | Feel and readability | **Shipped** |
| 5 | Know what you're restoring | Player-owned garden | **Shipped** |

**Why idea 3 was dropped:** the implementation only turned three of the eight puzzle-board kinds (classic, pairs, mosaic). The five newer board kinds build their own nodes and would have stayed upright. A setting that works on some boards and not others is a half-feature (taste charter rule 6), and the turn can't be judged without a tour. It also reused the take-turns icon in Settings. All changes were reverted. To retry it: give every `IPuzzleBoard` a named board root first, then judge a portrait, landscape and 150% tour.

**Idea 1 result (K5 gate):** `RootedBoards` generated a uniquely solvable 4×4 board for **200/200 seeds with one root and 200/200 with two roots**; the median solver count was 18 nodes. So C1 passes the automated gate, and generation is cheap. What's still unknown: whether a root stone reads as "something sits here" and not as a missing tile. That needs painted art and a greyscale readability check before any wiring.

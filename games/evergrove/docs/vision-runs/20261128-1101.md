## Ideas and outcome

| # | Idea | Area | Outcome |

| 1 | Do forked streams need real reasoning? | Puzzle depth | **Shipped as a hidden gate.** Answer: sometimes (see below) |

**Idea 1 result:** `ForkBoards` built a uniquely solvable 5×5 forked-stream board for all 100 seeds (0–99), with 4–9 fork tiles (median 6). Arc-consistency deduction forced 2,409 of 2,500 cells. 86 boards were fully forced, and 14 needed a deduce-then-guess search: 1 guess on 2 boards, 2–5 guesses on 11, and 6 on 1. Earlier runs found **zero** boards that needed a guess, across all authored boards and 400 root-stone boards. Forks are the first mechanic measured to break local forcing, though only on one board in seven, so they add only a little depth. Next step for depth: test whether 6×6 or fewer-hint fork boards raise that share, then draw one fork tile and check it reads at a glance (charter rule 6) before wiring anything.

**Idea 4 result (the cheapest disproof did not hold: there is a real problem):** WaterChannel `#18717F` and DryChannel `#9C5A38` are nearly the same lightness. Their contrast is 1.06:1 for normal vision, 1.18:1 for protanopia, 1.22:1 for deuteranopia and **1.00:1 for tritanopia**. They differ by hue only, so a player with tritanopia, or anyone on a dim screen, can't tell wet pipes from dry ones by the channel alone. Wet tiles also get a different tile colour and leak markers, so the meaning isn't lost, but the channel itself doesn't carry it. **No colour was changed.** A candidate fix is to darken WaterChannel to `#104B54`, which measures 1.83 / 1.50 / 2.06 / 1.76. It still needs a recheck of the 3:1 tile pairs and a look at the painted board, so it is left for an art review. The test locks today's distance (simulated RGB distance ≥ 0.2, lowest 0.298) and records the numbers. Restore a 1.5:1 assertion once a colour change lands.

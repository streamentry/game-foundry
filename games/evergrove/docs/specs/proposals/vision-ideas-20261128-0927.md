## 1. Forced moves, measured (puzzle depth)

- **Vision:** §2.2 "depth from a system, not content volume" (unsolved). `PuzzleProfile.forcedMoveCount` has been declared and always `null` since the diagnostics were written, so no board's logical depth is known.
- **Player value (later):** an editor can order boards so each chapter asks for a little more reasoning, and can prove whether a future system (root stones, ecology) adds depth or only looks different. Without a number, "harder" is a guess.
- **Strongest counterargument:** a hidden number changes nothing on screen; forced-move propagation is a proxy, not human difficulty.
- **Cheapest disproof:** if every authored board is fully solved by local forcing alone (depth looks identical everywhere), the measure can't separate boards and should be replaced by solver branching.
- **Files:** new `Assets/Scripts/Core/DeductionDepth.cs`; `Core/PuzzleDiagnostics.cs` (fill `forcedMoveCount`); new `Tests/DeductionDepthTests.cs`; update the `Program.cs` assertion that pins it to `null`.
- **Acceptance (pure C#):** for all 12 authored boards and 33 variants: deterministic; never contradicts the known solution; the count is between 0 and the number of rotatable tiles; a rotated variant has the same count as its base board (rotation keeps the graph, so this pins the honest "no harder" claim); the anchored tile counts as known from the start. The per-board curve is recorded in the run log.
- **On screen:** nothing.

## 2. The lantern by your own thing (truthful village memory)

- **Vision:** §3 "the place remembers it truthfully"; charter rule 5. The dusk lantern letter (checklist 54) is generic: "I thought of your garden".
- **Player value:** the letter names something the player actually chose: "I've hung the lantern by your quince tree tonight." A small, true proof that the village knows what you made.
- **Strongest counterargument:** repetition; if the latest thing never changes, the letter says the same name every evening.
- **Cheapest disproof:** in a day-2 to day-7 diary session, ask what the evening letter said. If players can't recall the named thing any better than the generic line, revert.
- **Files:** `Core/DayRhythm.cs` (new `LanternWords(int dayStamp, WorldModel world)` and `LatestThing`); `Unity/EvergroveApp.DayRhythm.cs` (the one call in `ShowLanternLetter`); new `Tests/LanternThingTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** the named thing comes only from the save (the latest saved village choice, through `GardenCatalog.For`); with no choice, or in Puzzle-only mode, the existing generic letter is returned unchanged; the same day and save give the same words; every named line is short (under 120 characters) and never mentions the player's absence.
- **On screen:** the existing toast beside the lantern token shows the new sentence.
- **Art/feel:** no new UI; a warm, short line in the letter's existing voice.

## 3. "The two of you" only when it is two (family play, one device)

- **Vision:** §1a, §4.4 rung 0; charter rule 5. Take turns supports up to four players (I303), but every keeper's remark after a shared solve says "the two of you" or "two heads". With three or four at the table that is false.
- **Player value:** a grandparent, a parent and a child who solve together hear "The three of you freed the water together", which is true and includes the third person.
- **Strongest counterargument:** few tables have three or four players.
- **Cheapest disproof:** in the family co-play session, count tables of three or more. If none appear in five groups, the extra wording costs nothing but earns nothing.
- **Files:** `Core/NeighbourNotice.cs` (`TogetherLine(kind, players)`), `Core/CompanionFacts.cs` (context carries the player count), `Core/CompanionSpeech.cs`, `Unity/TreePlay.cs` (expose the count), `Unity/EvergroveApp.Hub.cs`, `Unity/EvergroveApp.Map.cs`, `Unity/EvergroveApp.Neighbours.cs`; new `Tests/TogetherCountTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** for every puzzle kind, 2 players gives exactly today's line; 3 and 4 players give a line with no "two", "both" or "twice"; out-of-range counts fall back to the two-player line; nothing about who played is stored (the count lives in memory only, like the kind does today).
- **On screen:** the keeper's existing remark.

## 4. Four turn colours that still differ in grey (art and readability)

- **Vision:** §1a "any hand can help"; charter rules 2 and 4; checklist 92. The take-turns colours sage, honey, sky and rose (I264, I303) are close in lightness: sage and rose are almost the same grey, and sage and sky are close. In low light, on a dim phone, or for a colour-blind player, two turns can look alike.
- **Player value:** whose turn it is reads at a glance across a table, for every eye.
- **Strongest counterargument:** the pips and the flipped icon already say whose turn it is.
- **Cheapest disproof:** a greyscale and deuteranopia screenshot of the four tokens; if they already read as four steps, keep the old palette.
- **Files:** new `Core/TurnPalette.cs` (four RGB colours as floats and a WCAG relative-luminance function); `Unity/TreePlay.cs` (read the colours from it); new `Tests/TurnPaletteTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** each pair of turn colours has a contrast ratio of at least 1.3:1 in relative luminance; each keeps its hue family (sage stays green-dominant, honey yellow, sky blue, rose red). Proposed values: sage (0.42, 0.57, 0.43), honey (0.97, 0.87, 0.60), sky (0.64, 0.77, 0.91), rose (0.84, 0.56, 0.60); the smallest pairwise ratio is 1.36 (the old palette's was 1.03).
- **Art/feel:** muted watercolour pigments in four lightness steps, like a paint box: deep sage, mid rose, light sky, pale honey. No saturation jump.

## 5. The turn is spoken, too (accessibility)

- **Vision:** §2.7 "readable and touchable"; M5 accessibility gate; checklist 93. When turns pass, only colour, a flipped icon and pips change. A screen-reader user hears nothing.
- **Player value:** with VoiceOver or TalkBack on, the take-turns token reads "Second player's turn, of three."
- **Strongest counterargument:** screen-reader users playing take-turns may be rare.
- **Cheapest disproof:** a blind-tester session (already needed for checklist 93); if the label doesn't help them follow the table, remove it.
- **Files:** `Core/TakeTurns.cs` (`Spoken(turn, players)`); `Unity/TreePlay.cs` (`PaintTurn` updates one persistent hidden caption on the token); new `Tests/SpokenTurnTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** "Playing alone." for one player; "First player's turn, of two." through "Fourth player's turn, of four."; out-of-range input never throws; one caption node per token, reused, never a new node per turn.
- **On screen:** nothing visible.

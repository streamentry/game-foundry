## 1. The axle chapter's firm end, filled? (puzzle depth)

**Vision:** §2.2 "depth from a system… editorially reviewed difficulty curves". Run 1502 drew a 12-board axle chapter from seeds 0–59 with work 4,4,4,4,4,6,6,6,8,8,9,17: a real curve with a thin firm end and a jump from 9 to 17.

**Player value:** a future chapter whose last boards climb in steps a player can follow, not a cliff on the final board.

**Counterargument:** more seeds may only add more easy boards; solver work is not felt difficulty.

**Cheapest disproof:** if seeds 0–239 still leave a step above 4 between consecutive chapter boards, linked gates alone can't carry a firm end, and the chapter needs a second mechanic.

**Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/LinkedForkChapter.cs`; `Tests/LinkedForkChapterTests.cs`.

**Acceptance (pure C#):** new `LinkedForkChapter.LargestStep(int[] seeds)` (largest work increase between consecutive seeds; 0 for fewer than two; never throws on unknown seeds: treat a seed with no board as an error the caller can see, by throwing `InvalidOperationException` as `Work` does). The test locks the 60-seed chapter unchanged, then draws `Draw(12, 240)` (or the largest limit that keeps this test under ~15 s extra), prints seeds, work and spread, locks them, and records the finding: `LargestStep ≤ 4` means "filled", otherwise "still a cliff". Nothing visible: not wired into play.

**Art/feel:** none.

## 2. A chosen thing says which stream it shaped (truthful village memory)

**Vision:** §3 "your choice changes the next puzzle, and the place remembers it truthfully". Today the protected pipe names its cause while you play the next board (`LandmarkCause`), but once you move on, nothing in the village remembers that link. A thing's caption says when it was chosen, not what it did.

**Player value:** holding the quince tree on the map says "Quince tree, chosen after stream 3. Four streams have run past it since. Its roots held a pipe in stream 4." The village remembers the puzzle consequence, not just the choice.

**Counterargument:** the caption grows longer; few players long-press.

**Cheapest disproof:** if the save can't prove which board a choice shaped, the sentence can't be truthful and the idea dies. (Checked: `WorldModel.Choose` records choice *i* at round *i*, then `Advance` gives board *i*+1 the variant `VariantAfterChoice(choice i)`; the last story choice shapes nothing.)

**Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/ThingStory.cs`; `Tests/ThingStoryTests.cs`.

**Acceptance (pure C#):** for chosen thing *i* in a Living world, when board *i*+1 exists (`i + 1 < PuzzleCatalog.Count`), add after the "since" sentence: trees and flowers "Its roots held a pipe in stream {i+2}."; built things (bridge family) "It held a pipe in stream {i+2}." Present tense ("hold", "holds") while that board is the current one (`Round == i + 1`). The last story choice adds nothing. Puzzle-only and matched still give "". Finished and together sentences keep their order after it. Visible on screen: the long-press caption on a chosen thing (Living mode, English).

**Art/feel:** words only, one short sentence, the same verbs as `LandmarkCause` ("roots", "holds", "keeps"), so the board and the map speak alike.

## 3. Each seat's pip wears its colour (family play, one device)

**Vision:** §1a "any hand can help" and taste charter "the world explains itself". The take-turns token wears the current turn's colour, but the pips under it are all earth ink. Nobody at the table can see which colour is theirs until their turn arrives.

**Player value:** at a glance, every seat sees its own colour under the token: a child can say "I'm the blue one" before a word is read, and the token's colour always matches one pip.

**Counterargument:** four tiny coloured dots are hard to see; the position cue already works.

**Cheapest disproof:** a family session where nobody refers to a seat by colour.

**Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/TurnPalette.cs` (`PipInk`); `Assets/Scripts/Unity/TreePlay.cs` (`PaintTurn` pip colours only); `Assets/Scripts/Unity/TreePlay.Turns.cs` (`TableSolved` pip colours only); `Tests/TurnPaletteTests.cs`.

**Acceptance:** `TurnPalette.PipInk(turn, background)` returns the seat colour scaled darker (same hue: channel ratios kept) in small steps until it reaches at least 3:1 contrast against the background, or the colour unchanged when it already does; wraps like `Rgb`; never mutates inputs; never returns black for a valid seat. Tested against `CardFill` parsed from `MosseloraTheme.cs`: all four ≥ 3:1. On screen: the current pip filled, the others ringed (shape stays), each in its seat's ink; at a shared finish every pip filled in its own ink. Reduced motion unchanged.

**Art/feel:** inks are deeper versions of sage, honey, sky and rose, like the same paint laid thicker; no new shapes or motion.

## 4. Petals and leaves fall from your own trees (art and feel)

**Vision:** taste charter §1.5 "everything you make is remembered truthfully" applied to the picture, and §3.3 "seasons repaint the map". Spring petals and autumn leaves drift over every living garden, even one with no tree that blossoms or turns: a drift with no source.

**Player value:** the drift becomes a consequence. A garden with no deciduous tree has a still spring; each tree you plant adds to the drift, up to today's amount. Snow is weather and keeps falling.

**Counterargument:** an empty early garden loses a pretty effect.

**Cheapest disproof:** a stand-in player prefers the bare-garden drift and doesn't notice the link.

**Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/SeasonLook.cs`; `Assets/Scripts/Unity/GardenMapView.cs` (drift count only); `Tests/SeasonLookTests.cs`.

**Acceptance (pure C#):** `SeasonLook.Falls(VillageChoice family, int catalogueIndex)` is true for shade trees that are not evergreen (unknown index −1 counts, as today); `SeasonLook.DriftCount(Drift kind, int fallingTrees)`: petals and leaves give 0 for no trees, then `min(20, 6 + 4n)`; snow and none as today. The old one-argument `DriftCount` stays. On screen: in spring and autumn the number of drifting petals or leaves follows the trees on the map; reduced motion still stops the drift.

**Art/feel:** no new art; fewer pieces for one tree, the full drift for a grove.

## 5. A thing on the map says what it is (accessibility)

**Vision:** §2.7 "readable and touchable for the people we design for". Every grown thing on the garden map is a button named "On the map", and the screen-reader bridge speaks a button's `Caption` child or else its name. So a VoiceOver player hears "On the map" for every thing, and the long-press story is out of reach.

**Player value:** a screen-reader player hears the same true sentence a sighted player gets by holding the thing ("Quince tree, chosen after stream 3…"), or the family label in other languages.

**Counterargument:** screen-reader use in the audience is unmeasured.

**Cheapest disproof:** VoiceOver doesn't reach map items at all (then the label is moot until the map joins the published hierarchy).

**Files:** new `Assets/Scripts/Core/MapThingWords.cs`; `Assets/Scripts/Unity/GardenMapView.cs` (one hidden child per thing button); new `Tests/MapThingWordsTests.cs`, registered in `Tests/Program.cs`.

**Acceptance:** `MapThingWords.Spoken(caption, familyLabel)`: the trimmed caption when not blank, else the trimmed family label, else "". Never throws. Each thing button gets one invisible child named "Caption" (alpha 0, no raycast) holding that line; the node keeps the name "On the map" (DevQaTour finds it by name). A source check pins the wiring. Nothing visible changes.

**Art/feel:** invisible by design.

## 1. Do bigger forked boards need more reasoning? (puzzle depth)

- **Serves:** vision §2.2 ("depth from a system… unsolved today") and §4.2 (ecology that changes the board graph).
- **Observable player value:** none directly yet. It decides whether forked streams are worth drawing. Run 1101 found 14 of 100 5×5 fork boards need a guess after deduction, the first mechanic that breaks local forcing. If 6×6 raises that share well above one in seven, forks become the leading depth candidate.
- **Strongest counterargument:** guessing is not the same as interesting reasoning; a board that needs a guess may simply feel unfair to a 40+ player without undo-friendly exploration.
- **Cheapest disproof:** 6×6 boards show the same or a lower share needing a guess. Then size is not the lever and forks stay a minor addition.
- **Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/ForkBoards.cs` (edge size becomes a parameter; 5 stays the default and every existing result is unchanged), `Tests/ForkBoardsTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** the existing 5×5 assertions still hold exactly (same boards for the same seeds). A 6×6 run over a fixed seed range generates boards, each uniquely solvable, every solution a spanning tree; the test records generated count, fork counts, forced cells and the number needing a guess, and asserts determinism (same seed, same board). Nothing on screen.
- **Art/feel:** none until a fork tile is drawn and passes a greyscale glance test.

## 2. The thing's story remembers the table (truthful memory)

- **Serves:** §3 ("the place remembers it truthfully") and §1a ("That's the bridge you picked" — something to recall together).
- **Observable player value:** holding a thing on the garden map that was solved by taking turns says so: "Quince tree, chosen after stream 3. Two streams have run past it since. You solved it together." A family can find the things they did together.
- **Strongest counterargument:** one more sentence in a caption; families may never long-press.
- **Cheapest disproof:** in a family session, nobody long-presses a thing or reads the line aloud.
- **Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/ThingStory.cs`, `Tests/ThingStoryTests.cs`. The Unity map already calls `ThingStory.For`.
- **Acceptance:** the together sentence appears only when the save's together list holds that thing's choice index (`WorldModel.SolvedTogether`), only in a Living garden; never otherwise; it disappears after a reconsider (marks are forgotten by `ForgetGrownMarks`). All existing sentences unchanged. On screen: the long-press caption gains one sentence for together things only.
- **Art/feel:** no new art; the caption bubble already exists.

## 3. The token nudges whoever is next (family play, one device)

- **Serves:** §1a design rules 1–2 and §4.4 rung 0 (side-by-side play on one device).
- **Observable player value:** when a turn passes, the take-turns token breathes once (grows to about 112% and settles in under half a second), so the next person notices it's their go without reading. With gentle motion on, it only changes colour, as today.
- **Strongest counterargument:** a moving token is one more thing competing with the board; players look at tiles, not the token.
- **Cheapest disproof:** in a family session, the next person still has to be told it is their turn as often as before.
- **Files:** new `Assets/Scripts/Core/TurnNudge.cs` (pure curve), `Assets/Scripts/Unity/TreePlay.Turns.cs` (plays it), `Tests/TurnNudgeTests.cs`. `TreePlay.cs` is at its 1,100-line cap; any change there must not add lines.
- **Acceptance:** the curve is 1 at the start and at or after the end, peaks at 1.12, is smooth (no step larger than a small bound between samples), and is always 1 when reduced motion is on. The nudge plays only while playing together and only when the turn actually changes. On screen: the token breathes once per hand-over.
- **Art/feel:** scale only (compositor-friendly), ease-out, no bounce, no sound; the turn colour does the rest.

## 4. Wet pipes read as wet for every eye (art and readability)

- **Serves:** §2.7 (readable for the people we design for) and v1 item 92 (colour-blind pass).
- **Observable player value:** on the puzzle board, wet and dry pipe channels differ in lightness, not just hue, so a player with tritanopia or a dim screen can tell them apart from the channel alone. Run 1101 measured 1.00:1 under tritanopia.
- **Strongest counterargument:** the stripe pattern already differs (solid vs broken) and a darker teal may look heavy on the painted board.
- **Cheapest disproof:** a darker channel can't reach 1.5:1 against dry under all four simulations while keeping 3:1 against every tile state.
- **Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Unity/MosseloraTheme.cs` (a new `WetPipe` colour; `WaterChannel` is unchanged because icons, maps and marks use it), `Assets/Scripts/Unity/PipeGraphic.cs` (the board's wet channel uses `WetPipe`), `Tests/ColourVisionTests.cs`, `Tests/ThemeContrastTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** `WetPipe` vs `DryChannel` ≥ 1.5:1 under normal vision, protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia; `WetPipe` ≥ 3:1 on all six tile states; the lightest such darkening of today's hue is chosen, so the board changes as little as possible. On screen: wet channels are a deeper teal; the finished stream stays the brightest state.
- **Art/feel:** keep the hue of `#18717F` and only lower lightness; the bright finished-stream teal is untouched.

## 5. The spring says where the water must go (accessibility)

- **Serves:** §2.7 and v1 item 93 (VoiceOver and TalkBack finish a board).
- **Observable player value:** a screen-reader player who lands on the spring hears where the outlets are ("The water must reach row 3, column 3."), instead of finding them tile by tile.
- **Strongest counterargument:** small boards are quick to explore anyway; the label grows longer.
- **Cheapest disproof:** a blind tester finishes boards no faster and doesn't mention the outlets.
- **Files:** new `Assets/Scripts/Core/OutletWords.cs`, `Assets/Scripts/Unity/EvergroveApp.Layout.cs` (spring tile label, English only), `Tests/OutletWordsTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** for every catalogue board, the sentence names every end tile except the spring, in reading order, 1-based rows and columns; the spring is the first end tile, as `TileSpeech` already assumes. Other locales are unchanged. On screen: nothing visible; the spring's spoken label gains one sentence in English.
- **Art/feel:** none.

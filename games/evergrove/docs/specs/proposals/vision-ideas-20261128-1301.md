# Vision ideas 20261128-1301

**Status:** proposal, not player-validated. Written by the unattended vision-ideas cycle. Every idea is small, deterministic and testable in pure C#. None adds timers, scores, network, data about children or new assets. Earlier runs (0855, 0927, 1001, 1101, 1201) were read first so nothing here repeats them.

## 1. Linked gates: does a tie between far tiles add reasoning? (puzzle depth)

- **Vision:** §2.2 "depth from a system, not from content volume"; §4.2 deterministic ecology that changes the board graph. The 1201 run showed board size is not the lever and recommended "a rule that ties a tile to something other than its neighbours".
- **Idea:** on a 5×5 fork board, two non-adjacent tiles share one axle (a mill axle in the fiction): one tap turns both a quarter turn. A new hidden gate, `LinkedForks`, builds such boards and measures them. Not wired into play.
- **Player value (if it holds):** a board where the reasoning has to jump across the garden ("if this gate faces east, the far one must face south"), which neighbour-only deduction can't do.
- **Strongest counterargument:** a link adds information, so it can only make a board easier, unless the generator uses it to accept boards that are ambiguous without it.
- **Cheapest disproof:** over seeds 0–99, count boards that have two or more answers without the link but exactly one with it, and the guesses a link-aware deduce-then-guess solver needs. If almost no board needs the link, or no board needs guessing, the rule adds nothing.
- **Files:** new `Assets/Scripts/Core/LinkedForks.cs` (+ `.meta`), new `Tests/LinkedForksTests.cs`, registration in `Tests/Program.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** deterministic per seed; every generated board has exactly one answer under the link; the linked pair is non-adjacent and both tiles are non-symmetric enough to matter; the true answer is reachable with one shared turn count; the link-aware propagation never removes the true orientation; the test prints the measured numbers and locks them. Nothing on screen.
- **Art/feel:** none yet. If it survives, the axle would be a painted wooden shaft between the two tiles, still, with the pair turning together in one 120 ms ease (instant with reduced motion).

## 2. Every thing on the map has a true story (truthful village memory)

- **Vision:** §3 "the place remembers it truthfully"; charter rule 5. Grown things got a long-press story in 1101/1201, but nursery plants and things planted from the basket still fall back to the bare family word ("Shade tree").
- **Idea:** `ThingStory` gains two truthful sentences, both from the save only. Nursery plant: "Lavender, from the nursery." plus "You grew it from its puzzle." when solved, plus " You solved it together." when the together mark holds. Basket planting: "More quince tree, planted from your basket. It came from the one you chose after stream 3." English only; other languages keep the family word.
- **Player value:** holding anything you put on the map tells you where it came from, so the family can say "that's the one we grew from the nursery puzzle".
- **Counterargument:** more words on the map; the charter keeps words for names and memories. These are memories, shown only on a deliberate long press.
- **Cheapest disproof:** in M3, ask players what a nursery plant is; if the family word already answers it, drop the line.
- **Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/ThingStory.cs`, `Assets/Scripts/Unity/EvergroveApp.Map.cs`, `Tests/ThingStoryTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** pure tests for both sentences, the blank and out-of-range cases, the together clause only when `SolvedTogether(BoughtBase + k)` holds, the "grew it" clause only when `BoughtSolved(k)`, and Puzzle-only gardens (no together marks). On screen: long-press a nursery plant and a basket planting on the map in English.
- **Art/feel:** the existing long-press caption, unchanged in style.

## 3. The new starter is announced (family play, one device)

- **Vision:** §1a "any hand can help", §4.4 rung 0. Since 1101 the next board starts with the next person round the table, but nothing says so except the token colour.
- **Idea:** when a board opens together and the first move belongs to someone other than the first player, the token's screen-reader caption says "Second player starts, of three." until the first move, then the usual turn line. Visibly, the starter's pip gets the one existing soft pulse when the board opens (motion setting respected; nothing new with reduced motion).
- **Player value:** the family notices that it is the child's first move this time, without anyone reading the rules.
- **Counterargument:** the colour already says it; the pulse might be missed anyway.
- **Cheapest disproof:** in the family co-play session, see whether groups start with the right person on board two.
- **Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/TakeTurns.cs` (`SpokenStart`), `Assets/Scripts/Unity/TreePlay.Turns.cs`, new `Tests/StarterTurnTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** pure tests for every players × turn pair, alone, and out-of-range input (never throws). The caption returns to `Spoken` after the first move and after undo back to step 0 it says the start line again.

## 4. Turn colours checked for every eye (art and readability)

- **Vision:** §2.7 readable for the people we design for; family play needs the four turn colours to differ. 0927 made them differ in greyscale; nobody checked them under colour-vision simulation.
- **Idea:** measure each pair of `TurnPalette` colours with `ColourVision.ContrastUnder` under the four simulations. Lock the result. If any pair falls below the greyscale floor already promised (1.36:1), nudge the lightest possible step and record the new values; the pips and the swapped icon stay as the shape cue.
- **Player value:** a colour-blind grandparent can tell whose turn it is from the token's colour as well as its position.
- **Counterargument:** pips and the mirrored icon already carry turn without colour.
- **Cheapest disproof:** the measurement itself: if every pair already clears 1.36:1, nothing changes and the test just guards it.
- **Files:** `Tests/TurnPaletteTests.cs`, and `Assets/Scripts/Core/TurnPalette.cs` only if a pair fails.
- **Art/feel:** sage, honey, sky and rose stay; any nudge keeps the hue and only moves lightness.

## 5. The offered hint is spoken (accessibility)

- **Vision:** §2.7; M5 accessibility gate. On a co-player's turn the hint glows honey as a quiet offer (I302), but a screen-reader user gets no cue.
- **Idea:** the hint button's hidden caption says "Hint offered." while the offer glows, and nothing extra otherwise.
- **Player value:** a blind or low-vision player on a family turn learns help is there, the same way a sighted player sees the glow.
- **Counterargument:** one more spoken phrase per turn may be noise.
- **Cheapest disproof:** a VoiceOver walk-through; if the phrase interrupts the turn line, drop it.
- **Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/TakeTurns.cs` (`SpokenHint`), `Assets/Scripts/Unity/TreePlay.Turns.cs` (+ the one call site in `TreePlay.cs` `PaintTurn`), tests in `Tests/StarterTurnTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** `SpokenHint` returns the phrase exactly when `OfferHint` is true and "" otherwise; one reused hidden caption node, never one per turn.

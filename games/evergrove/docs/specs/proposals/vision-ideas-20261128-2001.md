## 1. An axle board you can turn (puzzle depth)

**Serves:** vision §2.2 "depth from a system", §4.1 the verb. Six runs measured the axle chapter (`LinkedForkChapter.DrawByLevel(12, 240)`) as a hidden gate; every log's next step is "put it in play for a blind read". Nothing can play it yet: there is no move model, only a solver.

**Player value:** a pure, tested play model is the rules half of a playable axle board. When a board is wired later, a tap on one end of the axle turns both ends, undo puts both back, and the board says solved only when the water truly runs. No UI ships in this run.

**Strongest counterargument:** rules without a screen change nothing a player sees; the blind read still needs a board view.

**Cheapest disproof:** if the drawn chapter cannot be solved by taps alone (a linked pair that needs different turn counts), the chapter is unplayable as drawn; the test would show it at once.

**Files:** new `Assets/Scripts/Core/LinkedForkPlay.cs`, new `Tests/LinkedForkPlayTests.cs`.

**Rule:** `LinkedForkPlay(LinkedForkBoard board)` copies `board.Board.Initial` (check the real field name in `ForkBoard`) into its own masks. `Tap(cell)` turns that cell a quarter turn clockwise with `ForkBoards.Rotate(mask, 1)`; when the cell is `A` or `B`, both turn together, once each. `Undo()` reverses the last tap (both ends for an axle tap); returns false with nothing to undo. `IsLinked(cell)`, `Partner(cell)` (-1 when not linked), `Masks` (a copy), `Taps` (taps not undone), `Solved` = `ForkBoards.IsComplete(board.Board, masks)`. Out-of-range cells throw `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`. `static int FewestTaps(LinkedForkBoard b)`: the sum over unlinked cells of the fewest clockwise quarter turns from the start mask to the solution mask, plus the axle's turn count once (the same k fits both ends by construction). Pure, deterministic, no clock, no randomness.

**Acceptance (pure C#):** tapping an axle end turns both ends and no other cell; tapping a plain cell turns only it; undo restores the exact masks; every one of the 12 `DrawByLevel(12, 240)` boards reaches `Solved` by tapping each cell its fewest turns (axle once per k), and `Taps == FewestTaps`; a fresh board is not solved; record the 12 `FewestTaps` values in the test as measured. Not wired into play.

**On screen:** nothing.

## 2. Neighbours name every new thing in good English (truthful village memory)

**Serves:** vision §4.3 neighbours who remember, taste charter rules 4 and 6 (adult, finished).

**Found problem:** `NeighbourNotice.Arrival` and `Waiting` glue a fixed "A"/"a" onto catalogue names. Mira greets a nursery oak with "A oak, new in the garden" and an olive tree with "Someone sent you a olive tree cutting". Fen says "A new sunflowers!" and "A sunflowers cutting came for you!". Orin says "There's a beehives in your basket". Eight of the 50 trees start with a vowel and most flowers and several builds are plural.

**Player value:** every line a neighbour says about a new thing reads as a careful adult would write it. Broken grammar in a "hand-made" world reads as machine-made.

**Strongest counterargument:** these lines appear only for nursery arrivals and family cuttings, a small share of play.

**Cheapest disproof:** run every catalogue item through both lines in a test; if none were wrong, the change would be zero. Several are.

**Files:** new `Assets/Scripts/Core/ItemWords.cs`; `Assets/Scripts/Core/NeighbourNotice.cs` (`Arrival`, `Waiting`); new `Tests/NeighbourWordsTests.cs`; update any older test that pins the old strings.

**Rule:** `ItemWords.Plural(string name)`: true for names ending in "s" except "lotus", "hibiscus" (treat "cosmos" as plural). `ItemWords.Mass(name)`: true for "lavender", "chamomile", "clover", "heather", "honeysuckle", "jasmine", "wisteria", "lily of the valley". `ItemWords.WithArticle(name)`: "an " before a vowel letter, else "a ". Lines:
- Mira (trees, always singular): `WithArticle(name)` capitalised + ", new in the garden. Give it a season and it will shade the whole bank."; waiting: "Someone sent you " + `WithArticle(name)` + " cutting. It's waiting in your basket for a good spot."
- Orin: arrival singular "That new {name}: I would like to see how it is joined."; plural "Those new {name}: I would like to see how they are joined." Waiting singular "There's " + `WithArticle(name)` + " in your basket, sent with care. Shall we find it a place?"; plural "There are {name} in your basket, sent with care. Shall we find them a place?"
- Fen: arrival "New {name}! The bees will find " + ("them" if plural else "it") + " by noon."; waiting "Cuttings of {name} came for you! They're waiting in your basket."

**Acceptance (pure C#):** for every item of all three families (`GardenCatalog.Get`), both lines of its keeper contain no "a "/"A " before a vowel, no "a "/"A " directly before a plural or mass name, no "it" for a plural; exact strings for "oak", "quince tree", "sunflowers", "lavender", "beehives", "windmill". English only.

**On screen:** the neighbours' arrival and basket lines read correctly. Not checked in a tour.

**Art/feel:** words only, same voices.

## 3. The postcard names what you solved together (family play)

**Serves:** vision §1a ("older players get a reason to reach out to family"), §4.4 rung 1 (no-server sharing).

**Player value:** the text postcard a grandparent sends to a relative now ends with what the family solved together on this phone: "Solved together: the quince tree and the footbridge." A concrete, true thing to reply to ("That's the bridge we did!"). Nothing is added when nothing was solved together.

**Strongest counterargument:** a postcard is spoiler-free and short; a list of names may feel like a tally.

**Cheapest disproof:** in the family session, ask a relative which postcard they would reply to, with and without the line.

**Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/GardenPostcard.cs`; new `Tests/PostcardTogetherTests.cs`.

**Rule:** after the stream line, when `world.TogetherCount > 0`, append one line "Solved together: " + names + ".\n". A mark `t < WorldModel.BoughtBase` is a grown thing: name = `GardenCatalog.For(world.Garden, Math.Min(t, ChaptersPerGarden - 1), (VillageChoice)Choices[t]).Name`, only if `t < Choices.Length`. A mark `t >= BoughtBase` is bought plant k = t - BoughtBase: name = `Nursery.Item(Bought[2k]).Name`, only if k is in range. Unresolvable marks are skipped. Names in ascending mark order, each "the " + name, at most three, joined "A", "A and B", "A, B and C"; with more than three, the first three then ", and N more" (N the remaining resolvable count). If no mark resolves, no line. No counts of people, no scores, English only.

**Acceptance (pure C#):** a fresh world's postcard is byte-identical to today's; one together mark → "Solved together: the X." with the real item name; two → "the X and the Y"; five marks → three names and "and 2 more"; an out-of-range mark is skipped; building the postcard never changes the save (snapshot identical).

**On screen:** the shared postcard text gains one line. Not checked in a tour.

**Art/feel:** plain text, no emoji added.

## 4. Every pipe stands off its tile, for every eye (art and readability)

**Serves:** taste charter rule 4 (readable for adults 40+), vision §2.7, checklist item 92.

**Player value:** earlier runs checked wet against dry pipes and the protected frame. Nobody checked that each pipe ink stands off the tile it is drawn on (WCAG 1.4.11 asks 3:1 for graphics). A water pipe that sinks into a pale tile under deuteranopia is harder to read for everyone with tired eyes.

**Strongest counterargument:** the pipes also have shape and the water wave; colour contrast is one cue of several.

**Cheapest disproof:** run the gate. If every pair passes, record the numbers and change no colour.

**Files:** `Tests/ColourVisionTests.cs` (extend, reusing its theme parser); `Assets/Scripts/Unity/MosseloraTheme.cs` only if a pair fails.

**Rule:** read `PipeGraphic.cs` / `MosseloraTheme.TileFill` to learn which ink is drawn on which tile state. For every (ink, tile) pair that can occur (expected: `DryChannel` on `DryTile` and `AnchoredTile`; the wet ink(s) on `WetTile`, `SolvedTile` and `AnchoredTile`), contrast ≥ 3:1 under normal vision and each `ColourVision` simulation. If one fails, darken that ink the least amount that passes (keep its hue) and keep every older colour test green.

**Acceptance (pure C#):** the new assertions pass; the lowest pair and value are written in a comment.

**On screen:** no change unless a colour moved. Not checked.

**Art/feel:** keeps the painted palette; a darker ink only if it must.

## 5. The almanac remembers a thing solved together (player-owned garden)

**Serves:** vision §2.4 players own their garden, §3.6 the almanac, §1a family memory.

**Player value:** the almanac is the book you keep and show. For an item this garden's choices grew whose puzzle was solved together, the keeper's line adds "You solved its puzzle together." after "You chose it for this garden.", so the book holds the family's part, read only from the saved together marks.

**Strongest counterargument:** the thing's map caption already says it was solved together; this repeats it in a second place.

**Cheapest disproof:** a stand-in reads the almanac and is asked which things the family did together.

**Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/Almanac.cs` (`KeeperLine(GardenItem, WorldModel)`); `Tests/AlmanacHereTests.cs` (extend).

**Rule:** in the loop that finds choice index `i` whose item matches, return line + " You chose it for this garden." + (" You solved its puzzle together." when `world.SolvedTogether(i)`). Nothing else changes.

**Acceptance (pure C#):** a chosen item without a mark ends with the old sentence only; after `MarkTogether(i)` (a Living world whose thing i is solved, as `MarkTogether` requires) it ends with both sentences; an unchosen item never gets either; the save is unchanged by reading.

**On screen:** one more sentence in the almanac toast. Not checked in a tour.

## 1. Root stones: a solver gate for boards whose shape changes (puzzle depth)

- **Vision:** §2.2 "depth from a system, not content volume" (unsolved); [depth-and-ecology proposal](depth-and-ecology-proposal.md) C1 and its automated gate K5 ("no solver run has been performed on any new mechanic").
- **Player value (later):** a tree choice could leave a root stone on a later board. Water has to go around it, so the board's graph really changes. Rotated variants never did that.
- **Strongest counterargument:** a blank cell may read as a missing tile; and the gate alone shows nothing to players.
- **Cheapest disproof:** if root-stone boards with one unique solution are rare or slow to find, C1 is dead before any art is drawn.
- **Files:** new `Assets/Scripts/Core/RootedBoards.cs`; new `Tests/RootedBoardsTests.cs`; one line in `Tests/Program.cs`.
- **Acceptance (pure C#):** for 200 seeds × 1 and 2 roots on 4×4, each generated board has exactly one solution under the root-aware solver; a pipe port facing a root or the edge is a leak; the stream covers every non-root cell; generation is deterministic per seed; yield and the share of boards whose layout differs from every rotation of a root-free board are recorded in the run log.
- **On screen:** nothing. Not wired, deliberately. A root stone needs its own painted tile and a readability test first (charter rule 6).
- **Art direction (for later):** a mossy river stone, darker than the soil tile, with a soft rim of moss. It must read as "something sits here" in greyscale.

## 2. The protected pipe names its cause (truthful village memory)

- **Vision:** §3 "your choice changes the next puzzle, and the place remembers it truthfully".
- **Player value:** tapping the fixed pipe now says *which* choice fixed it and *where*: "Your footbridge from Copper Run holds this pipe in place." Today it says a generic rule, so the cause-and-effect link is left to the player to infer, which is exactly what M4 measures.
- **Strongest counterargument:** more words on a board that should explain itself (charter rule 3).
- **Cheapest disproof:** in M3, ask "why won't this one turn?" If players explain it equally well with the old line, revert.
- **Files:** new `Core/LandmarkCause.cs`; `Unity/EvergroveApp.Board.cs` (the landmark tap in `TapTile`); new `Tests/LandmarkCauseTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** the specific line appears only when it is true: Living mode, at least one saved choice, and the current board variant matches the last choice (the same guard the journal uses). Otherwise the existing localized rule is returned unchanged. English only; other languages keep the existing localized rule. The board name is the board where the choice was made.
- **On screen:** the existing toast and status line show the new sentence. No new UI.
- **Art/feel:** unchanged gentle nudge and toast; one short sentence, under 70 characters for the longest board name.

## 3. Facing the table (family play on one device)

- **Vision:** §1a and §4.4 rung 0, side-by-side family play; the endgame charter §3.5b "one phone, passed hand to hand".
- **Player value:** two people sitting *opposite* each other (a grandparent and a grandchild across a kitchen table) can turn on "Turn the board for the person opposite". On the second player's turn the puzzle board turns 180° to face them, then back. Nobody reads upside down.
- **Strongest counterargument:** most families sit side by side; a turning board could disorient.
- **Cheapest disproof:** off by default; in the family co-play session, count groups who sit across and whether they switch it on when shown.
- **Files:** `Core/TakeTurns.cs` (add `FacingDegrees`); `Unity/TreePlay.cs`; `Unity/EvergroveApp.Settings.cs` (one device-only toggle); new `Tests/FacingTableTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** `FacingDegrees(turn, players, across)` is 180 only for two players, across on, second player's turn; 0 otherwise (alone, three or four players, setting off). The setting is a device PlayerPrefs value, never the save. Reduced motion snaps; gentle motion turns in about 0.35 s. Only the board turns, not the top row of controls.
- **Art/feel:** the board turns like a tray being passed: ease-out, no bounce, no sound beyond the existing turn cue.

## 4. The bloom follows the water (feel and readability)

- **Vision:** charter rule 3, "the world explains itself"; the solve should show the stream the player made.
- **Player value:** today the finishing bloom spreads by straight-line distance from the spring, as a diamond. It should travel *along the actual stream*, tile by tile, so the player watches their own route fill.
- **Strongest counterargument:** a subtle change most players won't notice.
- **Cheapest disproof:** a side-by-side clip shown to stand-in readers: can they trace the route from the new bloom and not the old?
- **Files:** new `Core/FlowOrder.cs`; `Unity/EvergroveApp.Puzzle.cs` (the `celebrateSolve` bloom delay); new `Tests/FlowOrderTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** for every authored board and all 33 variants, the solved stream's order is a permutation of all cells starting at the spring, ending at the outlet, each step a joined neighbour; for unsolved rotations, unreached cells get -1 and never crash. The Unity bloom delay uses the step along the stream. Reduced motion is unchanged (no bloom).
- **Art/feel:** same pulse and timing per step (0.07 s); a 16-tile board fills in about 1.2 s.

## 5. Know what you're restoring (player-owned garden, trust)

- **Vision:** §2.4 "players own their garden"; restore should never surprise anyone.
- **Player value:** before confirming a garden-file restore, the card shows both gardens side by side: "On this phone: Willowbank · 7 streams · 23 things · 12 days" and "In the file: …". If the file's garden is smaller, one plain line says so.
- **Strongest counterargument:** extra numbers on a rare screen.
- **Cheapest disproof:** in a restore walkthrough, ask what will happen before confirming; if answers don't improve, cut it.
- **Files:** new `Core/GardenCompare.cs`; `Unity/EvergroveApp.GardenFile.cs` (the confirm branch); new `Tests/GardenCompareTests.cs`.
- **Acceptance:** summaries come only from real save fields (garden name, streams restored, things planted and placed, days played); the "smaller" line appears only when the file has fewer streams or fewer things; no new state; nothing changes until the existing explicit confirm.
- **Art/feel:** two short lines in the card's ink at body size; readable at 150% text; no colour-only meaning.

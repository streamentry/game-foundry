## 1. The axle chapter drawn by level (puzzle depth)

**Vision:** §2.2 "depth from a system… editorially reviewed difficulty curves". Run 1601 found 197 qualifying axle boards in seeds 0–239 with work 4×78, 6×55, 8×40, 9×8, 10×3, 11×2, 12×4, 13×4, 14, 15, 17, but its rank-spaced draw still gave 4,4,4,4,4,6,6,6,8,8,9,17 (largest step 8). The pool has a smooth ladder; the draw ignores it.

**Player value:** a future 12-board chapter that climbs one small step at a time, so the last board feels earned, not a cliff.

**Counterargument:** solver work is not felt difficulty; a smooth work curve may still feel flat or jumpy to people.

**Cheapest disproof:** if a by-level draw over seeds 0–239 still has `LargestStep` above 2, linked gates can't carry a smooth chapter alone.

**Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/LinkedForkChapter.cs`; `Tests/LinkedForkChapterTests.cs`.

**Acceptance (pure C#):** new `LinkedForkChapter.DrawByLevel(int length, int seedLimit)`: group qualifying seeds (same filter as `Draw`) by work. If there are at least as many levels as `length`, pick `length` levels evenly spaced through the ascending levels (first and last always kept) and take the lowest seed of each. If there are fewer levels than `length`, take the lowest seed of every level, then give the extra boards to the gentlest levels first, one more per level in ascending order (next-lowest unused seed at that level; skip a level with none left), repeating until `length` or the pool runs out. Result ordered by work, then seed. Empty for non-positive inputs; deterministic; inputs unchanged. Test locks the existing draws unchanged, then locks `DrawByLevel(12, 240)` seeds and works and asserts `LargestStep ≤ 2`. Record the finding in the class summary. Not wired into play; nothing visible.

**Art/feel:** none.

## 2. The protected pipe names the thing you chose (truthful village memory)

**Vision:** §3 "your choice changes the next puzzle, and the place remembers it truthfully". Tapping the protected pipe says "Your shade tree from Bramble Run roots this pipe in place." But the player chose a *quince tree*: the map, the memory screen and Mira all name it. The board alone speaks of a generic family.

**Player value:** the board and the village speak alike: "This pipe is rooted in place by your quince tree from Bramble Run." The cause is the thing you remember picking.

**Counterargument:** a longer line in a status bar and toast; the family word was shorter.

**Cheapest disproof:** a stand-in player reads both lines and doesn't link either to the choice.

**Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/LandmarkCause.cs`; `Tests/LandmarkCauseTests.cs`.

**Acceptance (pure C#):** new overload `Line(VillageChoice choice, string boardName, string itemName)`: blank item name falls back to the old `Line`. Otherwise passive voice, so plural and mass names read right: trees "This pipe is rooted in place by your {item}{from}.", built things "This pipe is held in place by your {item}{from}.", flowers "This pipe is kept in place by your {item}{from}." `For(world)` uses `GardenCatalog.For(world.Garden, last, (VillageChoice)chosen).Name` when the specific cause is proven (same conditions as today; English only). New `Specific(world)`: the proven line, or "" whenever `For` would return the generic rule. Old `Line(choice, board)` unchanged. On screen: tap the protected pipe in Living mode (English).

**Art/feel:** words only; the item names already in the catalogue, no new strings to translate beyond the sentence frames.

## 3. Each seat is spoken by its colour (family play, one device)

**Vision:** §1a "any hand can help", "respect every age". Since run 1601 every seat's pip wears its own colour, but VoiceOver says "Second player's turn, of three." A child says "I'm the blue one"; a grandparent listening hears an ordinal nobody at the table uses.

**Player value:** sighted and listening players share one word for each seat: "Honey's turn, second of three." Whose turn it is can be said aloud in the same words the pips show.

**Counterargument:** colour names mean little to a blind player; ordinals are clearer.

**Cheapest disproof:** in a family session nobody uses colour names for seats; then revert to ordinals.

**Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/TurnPalette.cs`; `Assets/Scripts/Core/TakeTurns.cs`; `Tests/TurnPaletteTests.cs`; whichever existing test asserts the `TakeTurns.Spoken*` strings.

**Acceptance (pure C#):** `TurnPalette.Name(int turn)` returns "sage", "honey", "sky", "rose", wrapping like `Rgb`. `TakeTurns.Spoken` together: "{Name}'s turn, {ordinal} of {count}." e.g. "Honey's turn, second of three."; `SpokenStart`: "Honey starts, second of three."; capitalised name; alone unchanged ("Playing alone."); hint and solved lines unchanged; never throws for negative or large turns. The name must match the pip ink the seat is drawn in (same index into `TurnPalette`). On screen: VoiceOver on a thing's water board while taking turns.

**Art/feel:** the names are the palette's own words: paint names, not "player 2".

## 4. The river carries leaves only from your trees (art and feel)

**Vision:** taste charter §1.5 applied to the picture, continuing run 1601 idea 4. In autumn the map's river shows leaves afloat on every garden, even one with no deciduous tree, while the drift above now correctly stays still. The river contradicts the sky.

**Player value:** a garden with no turning tree has a clear autumn river; plant one, and leaves ride the water too. The map stays a true picture of what you grew.

**Counterargument:** a bare early garden loses a detail.

**Cheapest disproof:** nobody notices the difference in a side-by-side capture.

**Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/SeasonLook.cs`; `Assets/Scripts/Unity/GardenMapView.cs` (one line edited in place; the file is pinned at 2,362 lines by tests and must stay exactly that); `Tests/SeasonLookTests.cs`.

**Acceptance (pure C#):** `SeasonLook.River(Season, int fallingTrees)`: autumn with `fallingTrees <= 0` gives `RiverLook.Running`; otherwise the same as `River(season)`. Old overload unchanged. GardenMapView passes `FallingTrees(<the placed things in scope>)`. On screen: autumn map with no deciduous tree shows no leaves on the water; with one it does. Reduced motion unaffected (static marks).

**Art/feel:** no new art; the river keeps its plain running look.

## 5. Holding the protected pipe says why it stays (accessibility)

**Vision:** §2.7 "readable and touchable"; taste charter "the world explains itself". A tap on the protected pipe shows its cause; a long-press (and so a screen reader's hint text path) only says "This tile stays where it is. Opens up and right." The reason is out of reach for anyone who explores by holding.

**Player value:** holding the pipe says "This pipe is rooted in place by your quince tree from Bramble Run. Opens up and right."

**Counterargument:** two places say the same thing.

**Cheapest disproof:** long-press is unused in sessions.

**Files:** `Assets/Scripts/Core/TileOpenings.cs`; `Assets/Scripts/Unity/EvergroveApp.Puzzle.cs` (the `AttachLive` lambda only); `Tests/TileOpeningsTests.cs`.

**Acceptance (pure C#):** `TileOpenings.Caption(bool anchored, PipeKind kind, int rotation, string cause)`: when anchored and cause non-blank, cause (trimmed, ending with a full stop) replaces "This tile stays where it is."; openings appended as today; otherwise identical to the three-argument overload. The app passes `LandmarkCause.Specific(world)` (idea 2), so an unproven cause keeps today's caption. On screen: long-press the protected pipe in Living mode (English).

**Art/feel:** words only.
